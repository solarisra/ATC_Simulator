# ATC Simulator Marketing Strategy

Status: working draft | Updated: 2026-11-16

Related: [Business plan](BUSINESS_PLAN.md) |
[Product strategy](PRODUCT_STRATEGY.md)

## 1. Objective and positioning

Find an initial audience that enjoys the current playable simulation, convert
interest into demo use and purchases, and learn what merits further development.
Institutional discovery is a separate, smaller pilot effort.

Proposed positioning:

> An independently developed desktop ATC simulation in early access, focused on
> radar control, voice interaction, and configurable fictional traffic.

Use footage from the actual build. State its limitations and validated hardware/
operating systems. Do not describe it as certified, FAA-approved, a replacement
for approved training, or proof of controller proficiency.

## 2. Audience-specific messages

| Audience | Message | Evidence/content | Call to action |
|---|---|---|---|
| Enthusiasts | Control traffic in a focused radar simulation | Uncut gameplay and voice demonstrations | Try demo or wishlist |
| Educators | Explore repeatable exercises for classroom discussion | Exercise walkthrough and deployment notes | Request a limited pilot |
| Training professionals | Evaluate a supplemental practice concept | Limitations, instructor review, repeatable scenarios | Discuss evaluation needs |

Only advertise available capabilities as available. Mark tutorial/instructional
tools, weather, traffic snapshots, multiplayer, and additional playable locations
as exploratory or planned, as appropriate.

## 3. Channel priorities

Start with a small sustainable presence rather than launching every channel.

| Channel | Role | Initial cadence proposal |
|---|---|---|
| YouTube | Searchable gameplay, voice demos, development explanations | One useful video every two weeks; reuse short clips |
| X | Short development updates, gameplay clips, and simulation-community discussion | Two posts per week, with scheduled replies |
| TikTok | Captioned vertical gameplay clips, voice-command demos, and beginner challenges | One or two short videos per week, reused from recorded sessions |
| Discord | Feedback, announcements, and peer community | Scheduled check-ins with published response expectations |
| Relevant Reddit communities/forums | Targeted discovery and honest discussion | Participate regularly; promote only where rules permit |
| Opt-in email list/landing page | Durable launch/demo announcements | One meaningful monthly update |
| LinkedIn and direct educator outreach | Institutional discovery | Small, personalized outreach batch each month |
| Patreon | Optional recurring development support and supporter updates | One substantive monthly update; publish tier-specific benefits before launch |
| Kickstarter | Finite milestone funding and campaign discovery | Build a prelaunch page; propose weekly campaign updates and monthly fulfillment updates |

Add Shorts, Instagram, or Bluesky only if reused content produces qualified
interest without delaying development. Invite niche simulation creators
to try the demo; coverage is voluntary. Disclose sponsorships and material
relationships as required, and request honest feedback rather than positive reviews.

Discord is a community channel, not guaranteed acquisition. Do not create a
separate subreddit until there is enough activity to maintain it.

## 4. Sustainable content workflow

Suggested starting budget: approximately five hours per week, adjusted to actual
capacity:

- Two hours capturing/editing a useful demonstration.
- One hour answering community questions and sorting feedback.
- One hour participating in relevant communities or creator outreach.
- One hour updating the landing page, measuring results, or preparing email.

Capture one scenario session and reuse it for a gameplay video, a short clip,
and a screenshot with explanatory text. Use the short clip as a vertical TikTok
video and share a gameplay excerpt or screenshot with a concise update on X.
Keep these within the time budget by reusing content rather than creating
separate production schedules. Use captions so viewers can understand
voice/radar interactions without sound. Do not publish private participant audio,
identifying telemetry, or customer reports without permission.

Content pillars:

1. What playing the current build actually feels like.
2. How to get started, including voice setup and CPU/GPU limitations.
3. What changed, why it changed, and what remains incomplete.
4. Community exercises and feedback, shared with permission.

## 5. First 90 days

### Establish the evidence

- Create a landing page with accurate features, limitations, requirements,
  contact details, privacy information, and one primary call to action.
- Record a short trailer and an uncut session; distinguish working features from
  prototypes and mockups.
- Prepare screenshots, a concise description, and a creator/press contact kit.
- Offer a tested demo when packaging and onboarding are ready.
- Publish community rules, bug-report instructions, and support expectations.

### Learn from a small audience

- Invite external testers on each intended platform.
- Collect structured feedback on installation, first-session comprehension,
  microphone setup, enjoyment, and willingness to pay.
- Conduct educator/instructor interviews separately from hobby playtesting.
- Experiment with one message or channel change at a time.

### Prepare Patreon and Kickstarter launches

- Compare measured demo engagement and purchase/wishlist interest with delivery
  costs; there is no assumed minimum audience or guaranteed conversion rate.
- Use a Kickstarter prelaunch page to gather campaign followers while scope,
  reward costs, and delivery readiness are validated.
- Publish a finite funded scope and reward terms, not an open-ended feature list.
- Keep Patreon optional; do not make a one-time game purchase look like a
  recurring subscription or sell overlapping benefits ambiguously.
- Explain the distinction consistently: Kickstarter funds a defined milestone;
  Patreon supports ongoing development and specified membership benefits.
- Route relevant YouTube, X, TikTok, and community posts to the appropriate
  campaign or membership page, using one clear primary call to action per post.
- Define license eligibility, preview access, cancellation effects, and any
  cross-platform perks before accepting support. Kickstarter rewards must not
  depend on an additional Patreon membership.
- Track campaign interest and Patreon paid membership/net receipts separately;
  neither is guaranteed future game revenue.

## 6. Measurement and spending

Track a small funnel:

1. Qualified visits, by channel/campaign link.
2. Opt-in signups, wishlists, or demo downloads.
3. Successful first sessions and repeat play, through consented measurement or
   voluntary feedback.
4. Purchases, refunds, and support requests.

Calculate conversions using consistent cohorts/time windows. An email signup is
not a purchase, and a follower is not a forecasted backer. Storefront attribution
may be incomplete; report that uncertainty.

Start organically. Set a small fixed experimental cap before buying ads; record
cost per qualified action and, when attributable, customer acquisition cost.
Scale only when net contribution can cover acquisition and support. Do not use
earlier discussion's follower-growth or revenue estimates as targets or evidence.

## 7. Claims and trust checklist

- State Yuma's playable scope separately from Medford's map-only coverage.
- Explain that initial separation placement is not runtime prevention, and do
  not claim proven FAA compliance from passing internal tests.
- Label scheduled work, experiments, and shipped features distinctly.
- Confirm Windows/Linux/macOS and CPU/GPU behavior on distributed builds before
  advertising support.
- Do not claim rights clearance for every model, voice, or dataset until audited.
- Avoid manufactured scarcity, undisclosed paid endorsements, review incentives,
  and promises of guaranteed training outcomes.
- Publish changes to price, access, and update entitlements clearly.

## 8. Ownership and review

Initially the owner handles product messaging and community communication.
Contract editing/design only when it removes a measured bottleneck. Use original
or appropriately licensed music and visuals.

Review monthly: which channel brings engaged testers or buyers, what questions
repeat, and how much development time marketing consumes. Reduce low-value
activity before adding platforms or hiring ongoing marketing staff.
